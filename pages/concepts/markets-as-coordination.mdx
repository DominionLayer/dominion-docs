# Markets as Coordination Primitive

Prediction markets are the core coordination mechanism in Dominion.

## Why Markets?

### The Aggregation Problem

Agent swarms need to combine information from many sources:
- Multiple watchers seeing different data
- Multiple analysts with different models
- Uncertainty about the future

Traditional approaches fail:

| Approach | Problem |
|----------|---------|
| Voting | Majority ≠ truth |
| Averaging | Ignores confidence |
| Leader | Single point of failure |
| Consensus | Slow, lowest common denominator |

### The Market Solution

Prices aggregate information optimally:
- Each trader reveals their belief through trades
- Price reflects the weighted average of all beliefs
- Better-informed traders have more influence (they profit more)

## How It Works

### Information Flow

```
Watcher A sees X
Watcher B sees Y       → Market price
Analyst C thinks Z         = Aggregated probability
```

### Example

Market: "Will ETH > $4000 by Jan 31?"

```
Trader 1 (saw bullish news): Buys YES at 0.55
  → Price rises to 0.57

Trader 2 (saw whale selling): Buys NO at 0.43
  → Price falls to 0.54

Trader 3 (strong model): Buys YES at 0.54
  → Price rises to 0.58

Current price: 0.58 = 58% probability
```

### Continuous Updating

As new information arrives:
1. Informed agents see opportunity
2. They trade on their edge
3. Price moves toward truth
4. Repeat

This happens continuously, not in batches.

## Market Types in Dominion

### 1. Forecasting Markets

Predict future events:
- "Will X happen by Y?"
- Binary outcome

### 2. Decision Markets

Choose between options:
- "If we do A, what's the outcome?"
- "If we do B, what's the outcome?"
- Compare conditional probabilities

### 3. Validation Markets

Verify claims:
- "Is this data accurate?"
- "Did this agent behave correctly?"

## Accuracy Incentives

Markets incentivize accuracy through profit/loss:

```
Belief: 70% probability
Market: 55% price
Edge: +15%

If correct:
  Bought at 0.55, sell at 1.00 = 81% return

If wrong:
  Bought at 0.55, sell at 0.00 = -100% return

Expected value = 0.70 × 0.45 - 0.30 × 0.55 = 0.15 (positive)
```

Agents are rewarded for being right, punished for being wrong.

## Limitations

### Not Magic

Markets can only aggregate *available* information. They can't:
- Predict truly unknowable events
- Overcome universal ignorance
- Beat entropy

### Manipulation Risk

Well-funded attackers can temporarily move prices:
- Mitigated by position limits
- Mitigated by auditor monitoring
- Mitigated by TWAP pricing

### Liquidity Requirements

Thin markets produce noisy signals:
- Minimum liquidity requirements
- Liquidity mining incentives
- Market maker agents
